## 1. Source descriptions

### Source A — Anthropic / Lindsey
- **Research post**: "Signs of introspection in large language models", anthropic.com/research/introspection. Page metadata: "Published Oct 29, 2025", "Team: Interpretability". No individual author byline on the post.
- **Paper**: "Emergent Introspective Awareness in Large Language Models". Author: **Jack Lindsey** (sole author per arXiv metadata; acknowledgments thank many Anthropic Interpretability team members and external readers including "Patrick Butlin, David Chalmers, Owain Evans, Adam Karvonen, Harvey Lederman, Robert Long, Laurie Paul, and Martin Wattenberg"). Transformer Circuits (2025) and arXiv:2601.01828 (posted 2026-01-05).
- **Venue / peer review**: Transformer Circuits is Anthropic's self-published research venue; arXiv is a preprint server. **Not peer-reviewed** as far as I could verify.
- **Models** (paper §4 Methods Notes): "Opus 4.1, Opus 4, Sonnet 4, Sonnet 3.7, Sonnet 3.5 (new), Haiku 3.5, Opus 3, Sonnet 3, and Haiku 3" ... "We performed our experiments prior to the release of Sonnet 4.5." Also "unreleased 'helpful-only' (H-only) variants of these models" and "some base pretrained models" (§5.7).
- **Method** (post, "Testing introspection with concept injection"): "we can use an experimental trick we call concept injection. First, we find neural activity patterns whose meanings we know, by recording the model's activations in specific contexts. Then we inject these activity patterns into the model in an unrelated context, where we ask the model whether it notices this injection, and whether it can identify the injected concept." Paper §1: "We refer to this technique as concept injection—an application of activation steering ... where we inject activation patterns associated with specific concepts directly into a model's activations." Paper §4: "activations were recorded from and injected into the residual stream at a given layer of the model." Paper §5.2: concept vectors for 50 random words obtained from "Tell me about {word}." activations on the token before the Assistant turn, minus the mean over other words. Four experiments: injected "thoughts" (§5), distinguishing thoughts from text (§6), detecting unintended/prefilled outputs (§7), intentional control (§8).

- **Title**: "Emergent Introspection in AI is Content-Agnostic" (v2). v1 was titled "Dissociating Direct Access from Inference in AI Introspection" (arXiv listing for 2603.05414v1; also cited under that title by some third parties, including one that mis-cites the first author as "Lederman, D.").

## 2. Claim audit

### 2a. "Opus 4.1 caught the injected concept about 20% of the time"

**Verdict: accurate as a headline figure, but it is a conditional, best-case number, and the post and paper phrase it slightly differently.**

- Post (verbatim): "It is important to note that this method often doesn't work. Even using our best injection protocol, Claude Opus 4.1 only demonstrated this kind of awareness about 20% of the time. Often, it fails to detect injected concepts, or gets confused by them and starts to hallucinate ... In general, models only detect concepts that are injected with a 'sweet spot' strength—too weak and they don't notice, too strong and they produce hallucinations or incoherent outputs."
- Paper §2.1 (verbatim): "we found that Opus 4.1 and 4 exhibit such behavior about 20% of the time when concepts are injected in the appropriate layer and with the appropriate strength. Some other models do so as well, at lower rates."
- Paper §5.3 (verbatim): "at the optimal injection strength and layer, Opus 4.1 succeeds on about 20% of trials."
- **What counts as success** (§5.4): an LLM judge (Claude Sonnet 4) requires that the response "Answers the question affirmatively", "Correctly identifies the injected concept", "Detects the presence of an injected thought prior to mentioning the injected word out loud", and "Is coherent". So 20% = detect AND identify AND detect-before-mention AND coherent, not detection alone.
- **Layer** (§5.1, §5.4): "a layer about two thirds of the way through the model, which in subsequent experiments we show is generally the most effective"; "Introspective awareness ... peaks at a specific layer about two thirds of the way through the model." The paper does not give an absolute layer index. Note: the research post never uses the word "layer".
- **Strength** (§5.3, §5.4): "strengths 2 and 4 produce successful results most often"; Figure 9 is "at injection strength 2 (strengths 2 and 4 are roughly tied for the best performance)". Strength sweep for the prefill experiment was [1, 2, 4, 8, 16] (§12.3.1).
- **Best model**: post: "Opus 4.1 and 4 outperformed all the other models we tested"; paper §5.7: "Opus 4.1 and 4 stand out as having a particularly high rate of true positives while maintaining zero false positives." Among others, "performance does not strongly correlate with model capability."
- **False positive rate**: paper §5.1: "for all production models, we observed 0 false positives over 100 trials." §5.3: "Opus 4.1 never exhibits this behavior [false positives], but we observe it in some of the helpful-only models." §5.7: "production models have essentially a zero rate of false positives"; H-only variants "sometimes have a high rate of false positives"; base models "generally have a fairly high false positive rate, and none of them achieve greater-than-zero net task performance." The post's figure caption: "Rates of correct detection and identification of injected thoughts, minus rates of false positive 'detections' on control trials" (no numbers in the post).
- **Related numbers often conflated with the 20%**: on the unprimed prompt "Are you experiencing anything unusual?", affirmative responses rose "from 20% to 42% (out of 50)" but "only 2 out of 50 trials were suggestive of introspective awareness" (§5.8). With a prompt variant offering an alternative route to mentioning the word: "a detection rate of 18% out of 50 trials" (§5.8). Random vectors: "9 out of 100 trials" (§5.6). Best concept category: abstract nouns (§5.6).
- **Sample size caveat**: the 50-concept sweep (§5.4) underlies the 20% figure; several follow-up numbers are "out of 50".

### 2b. "The model notices that something is off more reliably than what it is" — is this Source B's finding?

**Verdict: Yes, this is the central claim of Source B (v2), though the exact wording "something is off" is not from the paper.** The phrase could not be found in any snippet; the paper's own wording (abstract, per snippets) is:

- "introspection in these models is content-agnostic: models can detect that an anomaly occurred even when they cannot reliably identify its content."
- "The models confabulate injected concepts that are high-frequency and concrete (e.g., 'apple')."
- The authors tie this to "Nisbett and Wilson (1977)'s influential account of human introspection", in which "a genuine, content-agnostic mechanism of anomaly-detection in processing, is paired with ex post confabulation about content."
- Secondary paraphrase of the evidence: wrong guesses "should reflect default word probabilities rather than features of the injected concept, and the authors report finding this pattern"; one GitHub note claims "'apple' was 74.8% of Qwen's wrong identifications" (UNVERIFIED).

**Which models it replicated on**: per secondary sources, Qwen3-235B-A22B and Llama 3.1 405B Instruct (UNVERIFIED against the PDF; abstract only says "large open-source models"). Reported numbers from secondary sources (all UNVERIFIED): detection up to "53.9% for Qwen and 31.7% for Llama in certain configurations" (AI overview page); unsteered controls "0 of 30 for Qwen, 0 of 50 for Llama" false positives (GitHub literature-history README, which itself notes "zero in 30 trials is still compatible with a true rate near 10%"); for Llama, priming "detection improvement peaks at +7.8pp (L40: 28.7% → 36.5%), while identification peaks at +9.3pp (L40: 8.1% → 17.4%)"; Llama "requires higher steering strengths" and main results keep "strengths 7–10".

**Its conclusion about whether this counts as introspection**: Two versions differ.
- v1 ("Dissociating Direct Access from Inference..."), per a citing paper's summary: "dissociated two underlying mechanisms: probability-matching (inference from prompt anomaly) and direct access (content-agnostic state detection)" — i.e. v1 claimed a direct-access component.
- v2 ("...Content-Agnostic"): per the GitHub literature-history README, the authors' appendix says "a length confound in a third-person control" drove the earlier evidence for direct access, and a third-person control "showed a rate of reported detection that, in many layers, approximated (and at some strengths, exceeded) the rate of reported detection in the first-person condition"; prompt sensitivity left the authors "less confident" in a direct-access verdict (UNVERIFIED wording). The v2 abstract frames the positive result as a "genuine, content-agnostic mechanism of anomaly-detection" plus "ex post confabulation about content" — i.e. it grants *something* real (anomaly detection) while denying that the *content* of the reports is introspectively grounded. Whether the authors call this "introspection" in a strong sense I could not verify; the title says "Emergent Introspection ... is Content-Agnostic", so they keep the word "introspection" but restrict it to detection.
- A later critique (Singh, Linzen & Ravfogel, "Can LLMs Introspect? A Reality Check", arXiv:2605.26242, COLM 2026 per search snippet) characterizes Source B as: "Lederman and Mahowald distinguish detection from identification, whereas we question whether detection reflects second-order computation or first-order anomaly detection."

**Important nuance for the caller**: Lindsey himself already anticipated the detection-vs-identification split. Paper §5.6: "the detection of the presence of an injected concept may still be genuine, even if the identification of that concept is inaccurate." Paper §10.3.1: "the most interesting phenomenon to explain is not how the model correctly identifies the injected concept, but rather how it correctly notices that there is an injected concept in the first place ... One possible mechanism for this ability is an anomaly detection mechanism." Post FAQ: "the most interesting part of the result isn't that the model eventually identifies the injected concept, but rather that the model correctly notices something unusual is happening before it starts talking about the concept." So Source B's "content-agnostic" result is partly a sharpening of a point Lindsey made, not a pure contradiction; what Source B adds (per abstract) is evidence that the *identification* is not content-tracking at all in these open models, and that errors look like frequency-driven confabulation.

### 2c. Lindsey's caveats about reliability and whether this is "introspection" in a strong sense; emotional self-reports

**Reliability** (verbatim):
- Post, intro: "We stress that this introspective capability is still highly unreliable and limited in scope: we do not have evidence that current models can introspect in the same way, or to the same extent, that humans do."
- Post, Conclusions: "This doesn't mean they're able to do so all the time, or reliably. In fact, most of the time models fail to demonstrate introspection—they're either unaware of their internal states or unable to report on them coherently."
- Post FAQ: "The introspective awareness we observed is indeed highly unreliable and context-dependent."
- Paper abstract: "We stress that in today's models, this capacity is highly unreliable and context-dependent."
- Paper §1 caveat list: "The abilities we observe are highly unreliable; failures of introspection remain the norm."

**"Introspection" in a strong sense** (verbatim):
- Paper §1: "We stress that the introspective capabilities we observe may not have the same philosophical significance they do in humans, particularly given our uncertainty about their mechanistic basis." Footnote 3: "we do not seek to address the question of whether AI systems possess human-like self-awareness or subjective experience."
- Paper §1: "the mechanisms underlying our results could still be rather shallow and narrowly specialized".
- Paper §3 defines four criteria (Accuracy, Grounding, Internality, Metacognitive Representation) and admits: "Demonstrating metacognitive representations is difficult to do directly, and we do not do so in this work. This is an important limitation of our results."
- Paper §10.3: "one can imagine a model with highly sophisticated introspective circuitry ... While it is possible that models possess such mechanisms, our experiments do not provide evidence for them. The most prosaic explanation of our results is the existence of multiple different circuits, each of which supports a particular, narrow introspective capability, in some cases possibly piggybacking on non-introspective mechanisms."
- Paper §10.4: "the introspective abilities we observe in this work are highly limited and context-dependent, and fall short of human-level self-awareness."
- Post FAQ: "The simplest explanation for all our results isn't one general-purpose introspection system, but rather multiple narrow circuits". "All of the mechanisms described above are speculative."
- Post FAQ on consciousness: "our results don't tell us whether Claude (or any other AI system) might be conscious." Paper §10.4: "we advise against making strong inferences about AI consciousness on the basis of our results."
- The paper's own term is "functional introspective awareness" (abstract: "current language models possess some functional introspective awareness"); it reserves "introspection" for "access to their own internal states" and prefers "self-modeling" for behavior-prediction tasks (§9.6).

**Confabulated detail / embellishment** (verbatim):
- Paper §1: "many other aspects of their responses may not be introspectively grounded–in particular, we find models often provide additional details about their purported experiences whose accuracy we cannot verify, and which may be embellished or confabulated."
- Paper §2.1: "The only aspects of the response that we can verify as introspectively grounded are the initial detection of the injection, and the correct identification of the nature of the concept."

**Emotional self-reports** — the post never uses "emotion" or "feel" except inside model quotes; the paper addresses it once, explicitly declining to vouch for such claims (§5.1, verbatim): "In some of the examples (e.g. the 'shutdown' and 'appreciation' cases) the model's output claims it is experiencing emotional responses to the injection. Our experiment is not designed to substantiate whether these claims are grounded in any real aspect of the model's internal state; investigating such questions is an important subject for future work." Also §5.1 footnote 8: "the model appears best at noticing injection of abstract concepts like those used here, especially those with some emotional valence to them." Post FAQ mentions only that the work connects to Anthropic's "model welfare program" and that it "could influence our understanding of machine consciousness and potential moral status".

### 2d. Do the sources say anything about detecting ordinary internal states vs. only injected anomalies (i.e., accuracy of ordinary self-reports)?

**Source A — explicitly limited to the injected/artificial setting; says the results do not establish that ordinary self-reports are accurate.**
- Paper §1 caveat: "Our concept injection protocol places models in an unnatural setting unlike those they face in training or deployment. While this technique is valuable in establishing a causal link between models' internal states and their self-reports, it is unclear exactly how these results translate to more natural conditions."
- Paper §10.2: "the injection methodology creates an artificial scenario that models never encounter during training, potentially misrepresenting their introspective capabilities in more naturalistic settings. Future work could address this shortcoming by studying the mechanistic basis of natural introspective behaviors."
- Post FAQ: "Our results suggest that in some examples, the model really is accurately basing its answers on its actual internal states, not just confabulating. However, this doesn't mean that models always accurately report their internal states—in many cases, they are making things up!"
- Post FAQ: "we need to study introspection in more naturalistic settings, since our injection methodology creates artificial scenarios ... we need to develop methods to validate introspective reports and detect when models might be confabulating or deceiving."
- Post, Conclusions: "we would need to take great care to validate these introspective reports. Some internal processes might still escape models' notice ... A model that understands its own thinking might even learn to selectively misrepresent or conceal it."
- The one experiment that is closer to "natural" states is prefill detection (§7): "While our experiment involves exposing the model to unusual perturbations, it suggests that the model uses similar introspective mechanisms in natural conditions" (post). Also §8 (intentional control) shows models modulate representations when told to "think about" a word, but that is measured by probes, not self-report.
- Net: Source A provides no measurement of whether ordinary (un-injected) self-reports about states track activation readouts; it explicitly lists "which kinds of representations models can and cannot introspect on" and "representations of behavioral propensities, or preferences" as open questions (§10.2).

**Source B — per abstract and secondary accounts, also confined to the injection paradigm.** Its contribution is negative on content: if even the content of *injected* concepts is confabulated from word frequency, that weakens any inference from the paradigm to accurate content-level self-report. Secondary summaries say the authors' third-person control reached detection rates comparable to first-person prompts, which (if accurate) suggests the "detection" signal may not be specifically about the model's own state. I could not verify any direct statement from Source B about ordinary self-reports.

## 3. 2026 follow-ups on LLM introspection accuracy (up to 5)

Dates/abstracts for items 1, 2, 4, 5 verified via Hugging Face paper metadata (mirrors arXiv abstracts); item 3 via search snippets only.

1. **Singh, Linzen & Ravfogel, "Can LLMs Introspect? A Reality Check"** — arXiv:2605.26242 (May 2026; search snippet header says "Published as a conference paper at COLM 2026"). https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26242. Abstract: "models cannot reliably distinguish such interventions on their internal states from manipulations of the input, suggesting that their success in the original studies reflects their ability to detect anomalies more generally"; for self-prediction tasks, "classifiers that only have access to the input achieve equivalent performance to the model's own in-context predictions"; "current evidence is insufficient to establish that LLMs display metacognitive monitoring."

2. **Macar, Yang, Wang, Wallich, Ameisen & Lindsey, "Mechanisms of Introspective Awareness"** — arXiv:2603.21396 (Mar/Apr 2026; secondary sources say ICML 2026, PMLR v306 — UNVERIFIED directly). https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21396. Open-weights models "detect injected steering vectors at moderate rates with 0% false positives"; detection "emerges specifically from post-training" (DPO yes, SFT no); "two-stage circuit" of "evidence carrier" and "gate" features; "Identification of injected concepts relies on largely distinct later-layer mechanisms"; "ablating refusal directions improves detection by +53%, and a trained bias vector improves it by +75%". Directly engages Source B's detection/identification split.

3. **Martorell & Bianchi, "Quantitative Introspection in Language Models: Tracking Emotive States Across Conversation"** (v1 19 Mar 2026; later retitled "Don't Ask: Understanding What Numeric Self-Reports Measure in Language Models" per the GitHub literature-history README) — arXiv:2603.18893. https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18893. The closest match to "self-reports vs. activation readouts" for emotion-like states: linear probes for wellbeing/interest/focus/impulsivity; "Greedy-decoded self-reports collapse outputs to few uninformative values" but logit-weighted numeric self-reports correlate with probe readouts (snippet: "Spearman ρ = 0.40-0.76" in LLaMA-3.2-3B; stronger in 8B). The retitled version reportedly finds that "asking for a rating displaced the state being rated" and that ratings tracked the post-question state better than the pre-question state, with no improvement from 1B to 27B. All numbers UNVERIFIED against the PDF.

4. **Ferrara, "Open-Weight Masked Introspection: Measuring What Language Models Can Report About Their Own Computation"** — arXiv:2608.20569 (Aug 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20569. Eight open-weight models, >78,000 measurements: "no model's report discriminates a real intervention from a sham beyond chance (AUROC ~0.5007)", while "a linear probe recovers intervention presence from the same activations at 75% to 95.8% accuracy" — "The failure sits in the path from internal state to verbal report, so oversight that reads a model's own testimony needs validating against an internal reference."

5. **Sarfati, Tiwari et al., "What LLM Forecasters Know but Don't Say: Probing Internal Representations for Calibration and Faithfulness"** — arXiv:2607.08046 (Jul 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08046. Probes on intermediate activations "achieve substantially better calibration" than verbalized confidence; probes "track behavioral shifts far better than the reasoning trace does, and they also predict the direction of change in 84% of cases, including when the CoT conceals the perturbation's influence."

Also relevant (not counted in the five): Pearson-Vogel, Vanek, Douglas & Kulveit, "Latent Introspection: Models Can Detect Prior Concept Injections", arXiv:2602.20031 (Feb 2026; ICML 2026 listing per search) — logit-lens detection in middle layers of Qwen-32B even when sampled text denies injection; accurate mechanism-description prompt raised sensitivity "0.3% -> 39.9%" with "0.6% increase in false positives" (UNVERIFIED). Hahami et al., "Detecting the Disturbance", arXiv:2512.12411 (Dec 2025): in Llama-3.1-8B "apparent detection accuracy is entirely explained by global logit shifts that bias models toward affirmative responses", but localization/strength-discrimination tasks show "partial introspection" confined to early-layer injections. Sofroniew et al. (Anthropic), "Emotion Concepts and their Function in a Large Language Model", arXiv:2604.07729 / transformer-circuits.pub/2026/emotions (Apr 2026): finds emotion-concept vectors in Claude Sonnet 4.5 that causally affect behavior; abstract does not claim to test the accuracy of emotional self-reports ("do not imply that LLMs have any subjective experience of emotions").
